What a decade of EdTech cost the classroom

Every tool promised focus. Most delivered noise.

Sustained attention is a skill, built the same way any other skill is, through practice without interruption. Chat, tabs, games, and a dozen other things compete for a student's attention, all one click away from the essay they're supposed to be writing. The device meant to help students do their work became the easiest way to do anything but.

A school laptop, mid-essay
  • A tab open to something that isn't the assignment
  • A chat window, minimized but blinking
  • Autocorrect and AI tools finishing the sentence for them
  • A backlit screen bright enough to strain eyes by third period
  • Notifications, always one buzz away
ThinkRite, mid-essay
  • One document, no tabs to leave it for
  • No chat, no accounts, nothing pulling their attention elsewhere
  • A dictionary and thesaurus, nothing that writes for them
  • A paper-like screen, easy on the eyes for hours
  • No signal in, no signal out, unless a school turns one on
What's actually on the device

Everything a writer needs. Nothing more.

Every feature here is built to help a student write, think, or focus more clearly. Nothing extra made it onto the device.

01

A paper-like screen

Reflective, not backlit. No glow, no glare, no blue light by the end of a school day, and none of the pull of a bright screen designed to keep you looking at it.

02

Dictionary & thesaurus, built in

Look up a word without leaving the page. No browser tab required, no search history to speak of.

03

No browser. No apps. No chat.

Not hidden, not locked behind admin settings. It was never built. There's nothing to disable, because there's nothing there.

04

An outline mode

For structuring an essay before writing the draft. Same quiet screen, built for organizing ideas instead of finishing sentences.

05

Designed for posture, not just typing

An ergonomic keyboard with comfortably spaced keys, on a stand that angles the whole device up so there's no hunching over a flat screen.

School add-on 06

Classroom Signal

Off by default. A school can turn on one connection, and only one: to whatever the school already uses, Google Classroom, Canvas, or similar, for turning in finished work. Nothing else gets through.

From blank page to finished draft

How a class period actually goes.

1

Open to a blank page

The device turns on instantly, straight into the document a student was working on. No login screen, no loading spinner.

2

Type, and look things up as needed

The thesaurus and dictionary are a keystroke away, so a hard word never means putting the pen down.

3

Turn in when Classroom Signal is on

If a school has switched it on, the draft syncs to Google Classroom, Canvas, or whatever the school runs. Otherwise, the device stays fully offline, by design.

Built for both

For the school, and the student.

For schools

Convenient to hand out. Nothing to police.

No filtering software, no monitoring dashboards, no IT tickets about students finding a way around the lock. The restriction is built into the hardware. Turn on Classroom Signal if you want syncing, leave it off if you don't.
